    Pc 69

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery. At that time the monks from the group of six lived with the monk Ariṭṭha, and they did formal meetings and shared a sleeping place with him. Yet they knew that he was saying such things, that he had not made amends according to the rule, and that he had not given up that view.1

    The monks of few desires complained and criticised them: "How can the monks from the group of six live, do formal meetings, and share a sleeping place with the monk Ariṭṭha, even though they know that he's saying such things, that he hasn't made amends according to the rule, and that he hasn't given up that view?"2

    They told the Buddha, who questioned them and rebuked them: "Foolish men, how can you do this? This will affect people's confidence." A monk who lives, does formal meetings, or shares a sleeping place with a monk who he knows is saying such things, who has not made amends according to the rule, and who has not given up that view, commits an offence entailing confession.3

    Synopsis — The group of six went on eating, meeting and sleeping with Ariṭṭha, knowing he had not made amends according to the rule and had not given up the view. The rule that follows is the second half of the machinery the previous rule set in motion: once a monk has been put out of communion, staying in communion with him is itself an offence.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "If a monk lives, does formal meetings, or shares a sleeping place with a monk who he knows is saying such things, who has not made amends according to the rule, and who has not given up that view, he commits an offense entailing confession."4

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu shares a meal, shares Dhamma, shares a sleeping place, or performs an official act with a bhikkhu he knows is currently suspended for holding such a view, he incurs a pācittiya."5

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should any bhikkhu knowingly commune, affiliate, or lie down in the same dwelling with a bhikkhu professing such a view who has not acted in compliance with the rule, who has not abandoned that view, it is to be confessed."6

    Brahm

    "Eating, sleeping or performing Sanghakamma together with a monk knowing that he is suspended, is an offence of Pācittiya."7

    Ñāṇatusita

    "If any bhikkhu knowingly should eat together with, or should live together with, or should use a sleeping place together with a bhikkhu who is speaking thus, who has not performed the normal procedure, who has not relinquished that view, [this is a case] involving expiation."8

    The rule has three verbs — sambhuñjeyya, saṁvaseyya, saha vā seyyaṁ kappeyya — and the renderings name them differently enough to mislead.9 Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translates each by what the word-analysis says it covers, so that the first is "lives with" (sharing material things and Dhamma) and the second "does formal meetings with"; his recitation splits the first in two, "shares a meal, shares Dhamma", and gives the second as "performs an official act".10​ 11 Thanissaro has "commune" and "affiliate", Ñāṇatusita "eat together with" and "live together with".12​ 13 "Lives with" thus names sambhuñjeyya in Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga and saṁvaseyya in Ñāṇatusita, and the safe course is to read the rule off the word-analysis's three heads, which are taken up below, under Effort.

    The object's name divides too: ukkhitta is "ejected" in Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga and Glossary, "suspended" in his recitation, in Thanissaro and in Ajahn Brahm, and "boycotted" in Ñāṇatusita.14​ 15​ 16​ 17​ 18 They name one act, temporary and rescindable. The phrase that does matter is akaṭānudhamma, "who has not made amends according to the rule", which the Vibhaṅga defines in two words, ukkhitto anosārito, "ejected and not readmitted".19 It means neither unpunished nor unreformed, but not yet let back in, and it is taken up under Object.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    This rule protects the Saṅgha's one remedy against a monk who will not give up a view that misrepresents the Buddha, and through it that monk's own way back. Pc 68 penalises the monk who refuses when the Saṅgha presses him; if the Saṅgha then ejects him, the ejection means something only if the other monks act on it. In the origin story they did not: the group of six went on eating, meeting and sleeping with Ariṭṭha, knowing what he said and that he had not given it up.20 Thanissaro reads the rule as reinforcing his advice at Pc 68, that a monk who will not respond should be suspended at once.21 The distance is not a punishment for its own sake. It is the ejected monk's duty, as Thanissaro puts it, to change his ways "so that he may be restored to normal status", and a Saṅgha that carries on with him as before takes away the reason to.22

    Thanissaro gives three factors: a monk suspended and not restored, the knowledge that he is, and one of the three acts.23

    Object

    The Vibhaṅga's object is a monk ejected for not giving up Pc 68's view, and its word-analysis quotes that rule's sentence in full; akaṭānudhamma it defines as "ejected and not readmitted".24 But a monk may be suspended on three grounds — for not giving up a bad view, for not seeing an offence, or for not making amends for one — and Thanissaro reports the Commentary's argument that a monk suspended on either of the other two fulfils the factor as well.25 The factor in Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual names only "the view", Pc 68's.26 What is required, on every reading, is an actual act of the Saṅgha, and the Manual says so plainly: he "has been suspended by the saṅgha through saṅghakamma."27 The Khandhaka records that act against Ariṭṭha and, after it, that he disrobed.28

    Perception

    After two rules in which perception did little, this one has the ordinary grid. A monk perceived as ejected is the pācittiya; if one is unsure, it is an offence of wrong conduct; and if one does not perceive him as ejected, there is no offence. A monk not ejected but perceived as ejected, or doubted, is an offence of wrong conduct.29 Thanissaro extends the same pattern to one's perception of whether the act of ejection was valid, and says plainly that "none of the texts mention the matter"; on his extension a monk who sincerely believes the act was botched commits nothing by eating with the man.30 The extension is his, and I would treat it as a reading, not a rule.

    Effort

    The first act, sambhoga, is of two kinds. Material sharing is giving or receiving material things; Dhamma sharing is reciting or having the other recite, with an offence for every line, or for every syllable if recited by the syllable.31 Ajahn Brahm widens Dhamma sharing to "conversing on the Dhamma or Vinaya", which goes beyond the word-analysis's reciting.32

    The second, saṁvāsa, is doing the observance day, the invitation or a legal procedure with him.33 Thanissaro's example is sitting in the same assembly to hear the Pātimokkha, and in BMC2 he states the consequence: whoever recites the Pātimokkha in an assembly that includes a suspended monk incurs a pācittiya under this rule. A few lines earlier, of the same phrase "anyone who recites", he explains that it "includes not only the reciter, but anyone who listens to the recitation", and if, as I take it, the parenthesis carries over, the offence catches the whole room.34​ 35 Ajahn Brahm notes one exception, the act that restores him.36

    The third is lying down ekacchanne, with the four timings: he lies down when the ejected monk is lying down, or the reverse, or both together, or each time they get up and lie down again.37

    Disputed — whether ekacchanne, "under one cover", means the same roof or the same ceiling.

    Thanissaro reads it as "one with the same roof", unlike the walled dwelling of Pc 5 and Pc 6, and reports the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī as holding that a monk under the same roof falls under the factor "even if one is lying in a room that is not connected by any entrance with the one he is lying in."38 Ajahn Brahm too has "under the same roof as him", though he gives it the meaning of Pc 6's "same place", which Thanissaro distinguishes, and Ñāṇatusita renders the word-analysis's phrase "under the same roof".39​ 40 The Manual takes the other side: ekacchanna "could in principle refer to either ceiling or roof", but "one ceiling" seems the more natural reading, since otherwise "one is not really sleeping together" and might not even know who was under the same roof; Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga, "under the same ceiling", has already taken it.41​ 42 The practical difference is large: on the Manual's reading a monk may sleep in the next room of a building from an ejected monk, and on Thanissaro's he may not. A monk who wants to be safe keeps to the stricter reading. The perception grid excuses a monk who does not know the man was ejected, not one who does not know he is there, so the Manual's worry stands. That is my counsel, not a settlement: "cover" will bear either sense, as the Manual itself says.

    Non-offences (Anāpatti)

    The Vibhaṅga states that the first non-offence clause holds whether or not one's perception is correct, and Thanissaro thinks the same principle "would seem" to apply to the other two, so that a monk who believes the man readmitted would be protected whether or not he has been.43 Ajahn Brahm gives the practical reason the case should be rare, that a community which suspends a monk should announce it to other communities, and thought the act itself rarely if ever performed today.44

    The third clause matters most, and it does not wait on the Saṅgha. Once the ejected monk has given up the view, Thanissaro says, he no longer fulfils the factor "even if the Community has yet to restore him to normal status."45 So the question a monk asks before he shares a meal or a roof with such a man is not what the Saṅgha has done since, but whether the man still holds the view.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • Dukkaṭa — where the monk has been ejected and this monk is unsure of it, and where he has not been ejected but this monk perceives that he has, or is unsure.46
    • Dukkaṭa (Parivāra) — for the effort, before the act is complete.47
    • The pācittiya multiplies: one for each line, or syllable, of Dhamma recited; one each time they get up and lie down again.48
    • Thanissaro's extension, which he states is in none of the texts: no offence where a valid ejection is perceived as invalid, and a dukkaṭa where an invalid one is perceived as valid or is doubted.49

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence "if he knows that he has not been ejected; if he knows that he has been readmitted after being ejected; if he knows that he has given up that view; if he is insane; if he is the first offender."50

    Related Offences

    • Pc 68 — refusing to give up a bad view: the rule that produces this one's object.51
    • Pc 70 — supporting an expelled novice: the same acts for a novice, with saṁvāsa dropped, since a novice takes part in no formal act.
    • Pc 4 — reciting Dhamma line by line with an unordained person, whose counting by lines and syllables the word-analysis repeats here.52
    • Pc 23 — exhorting nuns, where the "formal meeting of the community" a sick nun cannot attend is the observance day, the invitation or a legal procedure defined here.53
    • Pc 5, 6 — lying down in the same dwelling as an unordained person or a woman, whose walled dwelling Thanissaro distinguishes from this rule's roof.54
    • Kd 11 — the ejection of Ariṭṭha, and the observances an ejected monk keeps.55​ 56

    Related Suttas

    • MN 22 — Ariṭṭha's view and the snake grasped the wrong way: the sutta version of the story this rule's origin story continues (see Pc 68).57

    A note on sourcing

    The Story is from Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation alone, in the de-dramatised register, with the complaint verbatim and the interrogation and rebuke in the standing summary form. Ajahn Brahm's retelling says the group of six knew that Ariṭṭha "had been suspended by the Sangha"; that is the Khandhaka's fact and not the Vibhaṅga's sentence, and it is not carried. The Manual, the recitation, the class transcript (session 030, 16 July 2012), Thanissaro's BMC1 and BMC2, Ajahn Brahm, the Concise BMC and Ñāṇatusita were all checked; the Concise BMC condenses BMC1 here, carrying Thanissaro's extension as its own lines. The commentaries are known here only as Thanissaro reports them, and the class is not cited: Ajahn Brahm carries the rarity of suspension, and the rule's purpose is drawn from the Story and Thanissaro. MN 22 is quoted from Bodhi's translation.
