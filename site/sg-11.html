    Sg 11

    Story (Vatthu)

    At one time the Buddha was staying at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrel sanctuary. At that time Devadatta was pursuing schism in the Saṅgha, a break in authority. The monks were saying, "Devadatta speaks contrary to the Teaching and the training. How can he pursue schism in the Sangha?"

    But Kokālika, Kaṭamodakatissaka, Khaṇḍadeviyāputta and Samuddadatta said to those monks, "No, venerables, Devadatta speaks in accordance with the Teaching and the training. And he speaks with our consent and approval. He knows about us and speaks for us, and we approve of this."

    The monks of few desires complained and criticised them: "How can these monks support Devadatta's pursuit of schism in the Sangha?" They rebuked those monks and told the Buddha. He had the Saṅgha gathered and questioned the monks: "Is it true, monks, that there are monks who support this?" "It's true, sir." The Buddha rebuked them, "Monks, how can those foolish men support this?", and laid down the rule.1

    Synopsis — Four monks answer those who criticise Devadatta's pursuit of schism by saying he speaks for them, and the Buddha lays down the rule that the monks who side with one pursuing schism are to be corrected, and offend only if they will not stop.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "That monk may have one, two, or three monks who side with him and support him, and they may say, 'Venerables, don't correct this monk. He speaks in accordance with the Teaching and the training. And he speaks with our consent and approval. He knows about us and speaks for us, and we approve of this.' The monks should correct those monks like this: 'No, venerables, this monk speaks contrary to the Teaching and the training. And don't consent to schism in the Sangha. Stay with the Sangha, for a united Sangha—in concord, in harmony, having a joint recitation—is at ease.' If those monks continue as before, the monks should press them up to three times to make them stop. If they then stop, all is well. If they do not stop, they commit an offense entailing suspension."2

    Condensed recitation translation — "If that bhikkhu has no more than three bhikkhu supporters, they should be admonished up to three times to make them stop. If they do not stop, they incur a saṅghādisesa."3

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should bhikkhus—one, two, or three—who are followers and partisans of that bhikkhu, say, 'Do not, venerable sirs, admonish that bhikkhu in any way. He is an exponent of the Dhamma. He is an exponent of the Vinaya. He acts with our consent and approval. He knows, he speaks for us, and that is pleasing to us,' the bhikkhus are to admonish them thus: 'Do not say that, venerable sirs. That bhikkhu is not an exponent of the Dhamma and he is not an exponent of the Vinaya. Do not, venerable sirs, approve of a schism in the Community. Let the venerable ones' (minds) be reconciled with the Community, for a united Community, on courteous terms, without dispute, with a common recitation, dwells in peace.' And should those bhikkhus, thus admonished by the bhikkhus, persist as before, the bhikkhus are to rebuke them up to three times for the sake of relinquishing that. If while being rebuked up to three times they relinquish that, that is good. If they do not relinquish (that), it entails initial and subsequent meetings of the Community."4

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 1)

    "Should one, two or three monks who are followers and partisans of that (potential schismatic) monk say, 'Do not Venerable Sirs, admonish that monk in any way. He speaks Dhamma, he speaks Vinaya and he acts with our consent and approval. He knows that he is speaking for us, and that (which he speaks) is also pleasing to us', these monks are to be admonished by the monks thus: 'Do not speak thus, Venerable Sirs. That monk is not one who speaks Dhamma, that monk is not one who speaks Vinaya. Do not, Venerable Sirs, approve of a schism in the community. Let the venerable ones be reconciled with the community, for a community in concord, on complimentary terms, without dispute, with a common recitation, dwells in peace'. And should these monks, thus admonished, persist as before, they are to be formally admonished, up to three times by the monks so as to desist. If being formally admonished up to three times by the monks, they desist, that is good. If they do not desist, it entails initial and subsequent meetings of the community."5

    Ñāṇatusita

    "Now, there are bhikkhus who are followers of that same bhikkhu, [and] who are proponents of [his] faction—one, or two, or three—[and] they should say so: 'Venerables, don't say anything to this bhikkhu! This bhikkhu is one who speaks in accordance with the Teaching and this bhikkhu is one who speaks in accordance the Discipline; this bhikkhu speaks in accordance with our liking and preference [the Teaching & Discipline]. Knowing us, he speaks, [and] this suits us too.' [Then] those bhikkhus should be spoken to thus by the bhikkhus: 'Venerables, don't say so! This bhikkhu does not speak in accordance with the Teaching, and this bhikkhu does not speak in accordance with the Discipline! Don't let the venerables too favour the schism of the community. Let there be convening with the community for the venerables, for a united community, which is on friendly terms, which is not disputing, which has a single recitation, dwells in comfort,' and [if] those bhikkhus being spoken to thus by the bhikkhus should persist in the same way [as before], [then] those bhikkhus are to be argued with up to three times by the bhikkhus for the relinquishing of that [course], [and if those bhikkhus] being argued with up to three times, should relinquish that [course], then this is good, [but] if they should not relinquish [it]: [this is a case] involving the community in the beginning and in the rest."6

    The renderings divide as they do at Sg 10, where the verb for the Saṅgha's act is discussed, and agree on the rest. Two points are this rule's own. The recitation turns "one, two, or three" into "no more than three", which reads as a limit on the supporters; the Vibhaṅga's limit is on how many may be pressed together, and that limit is the rule's chief point, which the procedure carries. And the correction adds a line Sg 10's lacks, which the renderings give as "don't consent to schism" (Brahmāli), "do not … approve of a schism" (Thanissaro and Ajahn Brahm) and "don't let the venerables too favour the schism" (Ñāṇatusita): what the supporters are asked to withdraw is their consent.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    The rule protects the Saṅgha's unity from the support a schismatic needs, for no one makes a schism alone. When the monks objected that Devadatta spoke contrary to the Teaching, four monks answered for him, "He knows about us and speaks for us, and we approve of this", and the Buddha's question was about them: "how can those foolish men support this?" The rule meets them as Sg 10 meets the one they follow, with a correction before any penalty, and it adds the line their words call for: "don't consent to schism in the Sangha."7

    In everything but its object and the act corrected, this rule is Sg 10: the same three stages of correction, the same offences at the motion and the first two announcements, the same annulment, the same grid of legitimate and illegitimate procedure, the same non-offences.8​ 9 Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual gives it three factors: siding with a monk who is acting to divide the Saṅgha, "as in saṅghādisesa 10", the Saṅgha's act of one motion and three announcements, and the refusal to stop at its end.10 Only the first is new.

    Object

    The supporters are other monks who side with him, sharing his view, belief and persuasion, and who support him, praising him and taking his side. They are one, two or three, and they speak for him against those who would correct him.11 What is corrected is their siding and support, voiced as "Venerables, don't correct this monk."12

    Procedure

    The Saṅgha proceeds as under Sg 10, with a single limit of this rule's own: "Two or three may be pressed together, but not more than that."13 Thanissaro gives the reasons and draws the practical conclusion, that the supporters must be dealt with quickly, before the monk pursuing schism gains a fourth. One Community, four monks or more, cannot impose a penalty on another Community in a single act. The penalty needs the agreement of every monk present, and a fourth supporter sitting in the meeting can protest and invalidate it; Ajahn Brahm adds that the protest of the monks being pressed does not count, so three can be pressed when no fourth is present to protest. And, as the Sub-commentary points out, once the followers number four they can go ahead with the schism even while he is undergoing the penalty for Sg 10.14​ 15 The Story itself had four supporters, and Ajahn Brahm finds it perhaps not surprising that the Saṅgha at Rājagaha could not prevent the schism.16

    The supporters' way back did not close when the schism came. When Sāriputta and Moggallāna had led back to the Bamboo Grove the five hundred newly ordained monks who had followed Devadatta, Sāriputta asked, "Sir, may we reordain the monks who supported the schism?" The Buddha replied, "Let it be, Sāriputta, don't think of reordaining the monks who supported the schism. Instead, have them confess a serious offense."17 The man who led a schism is, by Ajahn Brahm's account, barred from the monk's life for good;18 those who had followed him returned by a confession, not a new ordination, and the way back the rule holds open before a schism was still open after it.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • Dukkaṭa — for a monk who hears the supporters and says nothing; for supporters who do not stop after the private corrections, after the corrections in the Saṅgha, or after the motion; and for those who do not stop when the procedure pressing them is illegitimate.19​ 20
    • Thullaccaya — after each of the first two announcements of a legitimate procedure; with the dukkaṭa after the motion, annulled when the saṅghādisesa falls.21

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence, read as under Sg 10, if they have not been pressed; if they stop; if they are insane; if they are deranged; if they are overwhelmed by pain; or if they are the first offenders.22

    Related Offences

    • Sg 10 — the monk pursuing schism, whose procedure, permutations and non-offences this rule shares.
    • Sg 12, Sg 13, Pc 68 — the same pressing, for other refusals.

    Related Suttas

    • AN 8.7 — among the eight conditions by which Devadatta was "overcome and obsessed", the last is "bad friendship", the part this rule's supporters play.23
    • Iti 18 — when the Saṅgha is split there are quarrels, abuse, ganging up and rejection, and "those without confidence do not gain it, and some of those with it change."24
    • MN 128 — the Buddha's verses at Kosambī: "Though the Sangha is being split / None thinks himself to be at fault"; the rule's correction is for monks who think themselves blameless.25
