    Pc 22

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery. At that time the senior monks were taking turns instructing the nuns. Then, on one occasion, it was Venerable Cūḷapanthaka's turn. The nuns said, "Today the instruction won't be effective. Venerable Cūḷapanthaka will just be repeating the same thing over and over."

    Soon afterwards those nuns went to Cūḷapanthaka, bowed, and sat down. Cūḷapanthaka said to them, "Are you all here, sisters?"

    "We're all here, venerable."

    "Are you keeping the eight important principles?"

    "We are."

    After saying, "This is the instruction," he uttered the same heartfelt exclamation again and again:

    For the heedful one possessed of the higher mind, For the sage training on the path to sagacity— There are no sorrows for such a one, The peaceful one, who is always mindful.

    And the nuns said, "Didn't we say that the instruction wouldn't be effective, that Venerable Cūḷapanthaka would just be repeating the same thing over and over?" Cūḷapanthaka overheard that conversation between the nuns. He then rose up into the air, walked back and forth in space, and he stood, sat down, and lay down there. He emitted smoke and fire, and he disappeared, all the while uttering the same heartfelt exclamation and many other sayings by the Buddha. The nuns said, "It's astonishing and amazing! No previous instruction has been as effective as this one from Venerable Cūḷapanthaka!" And Cūḷapanthaka kept on instructing those nuns until the dark of night and then dismissed them, saying, "Go, sisters."

    But because the gates to town were closed, those nuns spent the night outside and only entered early in the morning. People complained and criticised them: "These nuns aren't celibate. They spend the night in the monastery with the monks and only now do they enter town."

    The monks heard the complaints of those people, and the monks of few desires complained and criticised Cūḷapanthaka: "How could Venerable Cūḷapanthaka instruct the nuns after the sun has set?" They told the Buddha, who questioned Cūḷapanthaka, confirmed that it was so, rebuked him, and laid down the training rule.1

    Synopsis — Cūḷapanthaka, whose instruction the nuns expect to be as unvarying as ever, answers their disappointment with a display of psychic power and teaches on into the dark; the nuns are shut out of the town for the night and the laity draw the obvious conclusion, so the Buddha forbids instructing the nuns after sunset, appointed or not.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "Even if he has been appointed, if a monk instructs the nuns after sunset, he commits an offense entailing confession."2

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu gives the ovāda to the bhikkhunīs after sunset, he incurs a pācittiya."3

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should any bhikkhu, even if authorized, exhort the bhikkhunīs after sunset, it is to be confessed."4

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 2)

    "Exhorting the bhikkhunīs after the sun has gone down is an offence of Pācittiya."5

    Ñāṇatusita

    "Even if a bhikkhu who has been authorised should exhort the bhikkhunīs after the sun has set, [this is a case] involving expiation."6

    The renderings agree, and the one clause they handle differently changes nothing a monk must do. Sammatopi ce, "even if appointed", is kept by Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation, Thanissaro and Ñāṇatusita, and dropped by the recitation and Ajahn Brahm. Kept, it tells a reader that appointment is no shelter: appointed or not, the monk incurs this pācittiya, and the unappointed monk, by Pc 21's own rule, incurs that one as well. Atthaṅgate suriye, "after sunset", the Vibhaṅga defines flatly, "after the sun has gone down", and the phrase asks nothing more of a monk here; the same words carry a real dispute at Pc 6.7​ 8

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    The rule asks a monk to close his teaching of the nuns while there is still light for them to go home by, so that the instruction they come for never costs them their good name, for the confidence of the people and the well-being of the Saṅgha. Cūḷapanthaka's fault was not his teaching, which the nuns called the most effective they had heard, but its length: the town gates had closed by the time they left, and people said, "These nuns aren't celibate." The Buddha's rebuke names the cost, as it does at Pc 21: "This will affect people's confidence."9

    This is a lean mirror of Pc 21, and Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual refers its terms there in a single line.10 The object is unchanged: a bhikkhunī fully ordained by both Saṅghas, one or more, with wrong conduct for one ordained on one side only and nothing for a trainee or a novice.11​ 12 The non-offence clauses are Pc 21's word for word, and the appointment, the eight qualities, the procedure and the eight important principles are all set out there. Thanissaro counts the same two factors, object and effort.13 Three things differ, all of them in the effort.

    Effort

    First, the rule reaches the appointed monk. It opens, "Even if he has been appointed", and defines appointment in the terms Pc 21 uses for its negative.14​ 15 In the Story, Cūḷapanthaka was taking his turn in a rota of senior monks, doing exactly what his turn required, and the fault was the hour.16

    Second, "instructs" is not graded here. At Pc 21 instructing in the eight important principles is the full offence and any other teaching wrong conduct; here the principles and any other teaching are alike the full offence.17 Thanissaro's summary is "any topic at all".18 After sunset, an ordinary Dhamma talk is the full offence.

    Third, perception follows the pattern Thanissaro sets out at Pc 4. If the sun has set, the offence is full whether the monk perceives that it has, is unsure, or thinks it has not; if it has not set, wrong conduct if he thinks it has or is unsure, and no offence only when he rightly sees that it has not.19​ 20 Unlike Pc 21's series, this one says nothing about whether the nuns' assembly is complete.

    The origin story is about appearances, then, but Thanissaro will not let the rule be read as an instrument of the laity's fear. Although the story suggests it is unwise in any case to teach nuns after dark, "the non-offense clauses give more respect to the bhikkhunīs' desire for instruction than to the fear of gossiping lay people."21 A nun who asks a question after sunset may be answered on any topic, one who asks for a recitation may have it, and one who overhears a teaching given to others puts no one at fault.22 What the rule forbids is the monk's own initiative at that hour.

    The nuns expected an ineffective instruction and were given the most effective one they had ever heard, by the monk whose turn it was. The rule that came of it is about the clock: no teaching, however good, is worth a nun's night outside the gates.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • A dukkaṭa for instructing a nun who is fully ordained on one side only.23
    • A dukkaṭa where the sun has not set but the monk thinks it has, or is unsure.24

    There is no dukkaṭa here for teaching something other than the eight important principles, as there is at Pc 21; under this rule that is the full offence.25

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence if he recites; if he tests them; if he recites when asked to do so; if he asks a question; if he replies when asked a question; if he is speaking for the benefit of someone else and the nuns listen in; if it is to a trainee nun; if it is to a novice nun; if he is insane; if he is the first offender.26 The list is Pc 21's, and its glosses are given there.

    Related Offences

    • Pc 21 — the anchor: the object, the appointment, the eight qualities, the procedure and the eight important principles; an unappointed monk teaching after sunset incurs both rules.
    • Pc 23 — instructing the nuns at their dwelling place; with Pc 21 and this rule, the three ways the occasion can fail: no appointment, the wrong hour, the wrong place.
    • Pc 6 — lying down in the same dwelling as a woman, where the same phrase atthaṅgate suriye is disputed.
    • Pc 7 — the five-or-six-sentence limit on teaching a woman, whose exemption for answering a question this rule shares.

    A note on sourcing

    The Story is from Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation alone, with the verse in his wording. Ajahn Brahm's telling has Cūḷapanthaka "always" giving the same sermon, where the Vibhaṅga has the nuns predict it on the day, and is not carried. Brahmāli's recitation, the Manual and the class transcript (session 023, 10 May 2012), Thanissaro's BMC1, Ajahn Brahm's Vinaya Notes, the Concise BMC and Ñāṇatusita were checked. Ajahn Brahm's factor analysis and non-offence paragraph reproduce BMC1 and are cited to Thanissaro. The class treats the rule as a follow-up of Pc 21 and adds nothing a written source does not carry.
