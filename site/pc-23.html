    Pc 23

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying in the Sakyan country in the Banyan Tree Monastery at Kapilavatthu. At that time the monks from the group of six went to the nuns' dwelling place and instructed the nuns from the group of six.

    Soon afterwards, other nuns said to the nuns from the group of six, "Come, venerables, let's go to the instruction."

    "There's no need. The monks from the group of six came and instructed us right here."

    The nuns complained and criticised the monks from the group of six: "How could the monks from the group of six go and instruct the nuns at their dwelling place?" Then those nuns told the monks. The monks of few desires complained and criticised those monks, and told the Buddha. He questioned them, confirmed that it was so, rebuked them, and laid down a preliminary ruling: "If a monk goes to the nuns' dwelling place, and then instructs them, he commits an offense entailing confession."1

    Soon afterwards Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī became sick. The senior monks went to see her and said, "We hope you're bearing up, Gotamī, we hope you're getting better?"

    "I'm not bearing up, venerables, and I'm not getting better. Please give me a teaching."

    "It's not allowable for us to go and teach the nuns at their dwelling place." And being afraid of wrongdoing, they did not teach her.

    Soon afterwards, after robing up in the morning, the Buddha took his bowl and robe and went to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, where he sat down on the prepared seat. He said to her, "I hope you're bearing up, Gotamī, I hope you're getting better?"

    "Previously, sir, the senior monks would come and teach me, and because of that I would be comfortable. But now that this has been prohibited by the Buddha, they don't teach because they're afraid of wrongdoing. Because of that I'm not comfortable."

    After instructing, inspiring, and gladdening her with a teaching, the Buddha got up from his seat and left. Soon afterwards he gave a teaching and addressed the monks: "Monks, I allow you to go and instruct a sick nun at her dwelling place." He then amended the training rule to its final form.2

    Synopsis — The group of six instruct the group-of-six nuns where they live, and the other nuns object; the Buddha forbids instructing the nuns at their dwelling place, and then, finding that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī has been left without teaching in her illness because the senior monks were afraid of wrongdoing, adds the exemption for a sick nun.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "If a monk goes to the nuns' dwelling place and then instructs them, except on an appropriate occasion, he commits an offense entailing confession. This is the appropriate occasion: a nun is sick."3

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu gives the ovāda to the bhikkhunīs at their dwelling place, except if a bhikkhunī is sick, he incurs a pācittiya."4

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should any bhikkhu, having gone to the bhikkhunīs' quarters, exhort the bhikkhunīs — except at the proper occasion — it is to be confessed. Here the proper occasion is this: A bhikkhunī is ill. This is the proper occasion here."5

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 2)

    "Exhorting the bhikkhunīs in their own quarters, unless the bhikkhunī is too sick to go to the monks' residence, is an offence of Pācittiya."6

    Ñāṇatusita

    "If any bhikkhu, having approached the bhikkhunī-quarters, should exhort the bhikkhunīs, except at the [right] occasion, [this is a case] involving expiation. Here the occasion is this: a bhikkhunī is sick; this is the occasion here."7

    The renderings agree on the rule. Two differences are worth naming, and neither changes it. Upasaṅkamitvā, "having gone to", is kept by Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation, Thanissaro and Ñāṇatusita, and dropped by the recitation and Ajahn Brahm. The going is implied in any case, but the word also marks the offence as one of going, which the Vibhaṅga defines on its own, "goes there", and it is in the going that the rule's point lies, as discussed below under Effort.8 And upassaya is "dwelling place", "quarters" or "residence", each narrower than the Vibhaṅga's own definition, taken up under Object.9

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    This rule keeps a monk's teaching of the nuns within the occasion the Saṅgha has arranged for it, which the nuns come to, and out of visits of his own to where they live, for the harmony of both Saṅghas and the restraint of monks like the group of six. The first sub-story shows what is lost otherwise: taught at home by the monks of the group of six, the nuns of the group of six told the others there was "no need" to go to the instruction, and it was the nuns who complained.10

    This is the third of the chapter's rules on the instruction, and the last of its mirrors of Pc 21: the appointment, the eight qualities, the procedure, the eight important principles and the non-offence clauses are set out there, and Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual refers "teaches" to Pc 21.11 Thanissaro gives two factors, object and effort.12

    Object

    A bhikkhunī, fully ordained by both Saṅghas, at her dwelling place. Wrong conduct attaches to one ordained on one side only, and nothing to a trainee or a novice.13​ 14 The dwelling place is defined generously: "wherever nuns stay, even for a single night."15 Brahmāli's endnote takes that to span anything from a single hut to a monastery.16 The rule marks out the nuns' own ground, not a building.

    The rule's occasion is a sick nun, defined as one "not able to go to the instruction or to a formal meeting of the community".17 Brahmāli's endnote identifies the meeting from Pc 69, where it is the observance day, the invitation ceremony or a legal procedure, and the Manual reads it as any meeting the nuns hold with the monks, such as the invitation ceremony.18​ 19 The test is whether her illness keeps her from the occasions on which she would be taught; how ill she feels does not decide it. Thanissaro places the illness inside the object, "a bhikkhunī who is not ill"; the Vibhaṅga makes it the rule's exception; the result is the same.20

    Effort

    To go to her dwelling place and instruct her. The verb is graded as at Pc 21: instructing in the eight important principles is the full offence, and another teaching wrong conduct.21​ 22 Perception follows Pc 4's pattern, applied to her ordination: if she is fully ordained, the offence is full whatever the monk believes; if she is not, wrong conduct if he takes her to be or is unsure, and no offence only if he rightly takes her not to be.23​ 24

    What the rule is aimed at is the monk who goes to the nuns and teaches them unasked; Thanissaro's summary excepts the nun who "has requested the instruction".25 The proper routes leave the initiative with others. Either the Saṅgha appoints him and the nuns come to him, as at Pc 21, or the nuns ask him to, which the Manual counts among the exemptions ("teaching when asked", its summary of the clauses for reciting when asked and answering a question).26​ 27 Appointment authorises a monk to give the instruction when the nuns come for it; it gives him no standing to go where they live and teach them there.

    The second sub-story corrects a different fault. The senior monks who visit Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī in her illness do exactly what the new rule says, and, afraid of wrongdoing, do not teach her. Neither the Vibhaṅga nor the Buddha rebukes them. What it records is the cost, in her own words: before, the senior monks would teach her and she would be comfortable; now they are afraid, and she is not. The Buddha goes to her himself, teaches her, and then widens the rule.28 Thanissaro reads the non-offence clauses the same way: "a bhikkhunī's desire for instruction is considered more important than the suspicions of the laity."29 The rule that the group of six earned by teaching the nuns where they lived would, unamended, have left a sick nun untaught; it is kept carefully, and not so carefully that someone in need goes without the Dhamma.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • A dukkaṭa for going to the dwelling place and instructing with a teaching other than the eight important principles.30
    • A dukkaṭa for instructing a nun fully ordained on one side only.31
    • A dukkaṭa where the woman is not fully ordained but the monk takes her to be, or is unsure.32

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence if it is an appropriate occasion; if he recites; if he tests them; if he recites when asked to do so; if he asks a question; if he replies when asked a question; if he is speaking for the benefit of someone else and the nuns listen in; if it is to a trainee nun; if it is to a novice nun; if he is insane; if he is the first offender.33 Apart from the sick nun the list is Pc 21's, as Thanissaro says, and its glosses are given there.34

    Related Offences

    • Pc 21 — the anchor: the appointment, the eight qualities, the procedure, the eight important principles and the definition of instructing.
    • Pc 22 — instructing after sunset; with Pc 21 and this rule, the three ways the occasion can fail: no appointment, the wrong hour, the wrong place.
    • Pc 7 — the five-or-six-sentence limit on teaching a woman, whose exemption for answering a question this rule shares.
    • Pc 69 — where the "formal meeting of the community" a sick nun cannot attend is defined.

    Related Suttas

    • AN 4.159 — a nun sends word that she is "sick, afflicted, gravely ill" and asks Ānanda to visit her "in the bhikkhunis' quarters", and he goes and teaches her, and she confesses "a transgression" in how she behaved: the sick nun's summons the rule's exemption allows, and the wrong it can be turned to.35
