    NP 24

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery, and he had allowed the rainy-season robe for the monks. Knowing that this was the case, the monks from the group of six went looking for cloth for their rainy-season robes in advance. And after sewing them in advance, they wore them. Then, because their rainy-season robes were worn out, they bathed naked in the rain.

    The monks of few desires complained and criticised them, "How could the monks from the group of six go looking for cloth for their rainy-season robes in advance, sew them in advance, and then wear them, and then, because their rainy-season robes are worn out, bathe naked in the rain?" After rebuking them in many ways, they told the Buddha. He had the Saṅgha gathered and questioned the monks: "Is it true, monks, that you did this?" "It's true, sir." The Buddha rebuked them: "Foolish men, how could you do this? This will affect people's confidence …" And he laid down the rule.1

    Synopsis — the group of six seek and sew their rainy-season robes so far in advance that the robes are worn out when the rains come, and they bathe naked in the rain; the Buddha sets when the cloth may be sought, in the last month of the hot season, and when it may be worn, in its last half-month.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "When there is a month left of summer, a monk may go looking for cloth for his rainy-season robe. When there is a half-month left, he may sew it and then wear it. If he goes looking for cloth for his rainy-season robe when there is more than a month left of summer, or if he sews it and then wears it when there is more than a half-month left, he commits an offense entailing relinquishment and confession."2

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu seeks a rainy-season robe when there is more than a month left of the hot season or if he wears one when there is more than a fortnight left, he incurs a nissaggiya pācittiya."3

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "When a month is left to the hot season, a bhikkhu may seek a rains-bathing cloth. When a half-month is left to the hot season, (the cloth) having been made, may be worn. If when more than a month is left to the hot season he should seek a rains-bathing cloth, (or) when more than a half-month is left to the hot season, (the cloth) having been made should be worn, it is to be forfeited and confessed."4

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 1)

    "The time for seeking a rains-cloth is the last month of the hot-season and the four months of the rainy-season. Receiving a rains-cloth, which one went seeking for outside of this time, is a nissaggiya pācittiya. The time for wearing a rains-cloth is the last fortnight of the hot-season and the four months of the rainy-season. Wearing a rains-cloth outside of this time is a nissaggiya pācittiya."5

    Ñāṇatusita

    "[Thinking:] 'One month is what remains of the hot season,' [then] the robe-cloth for the rain's bathing-cloth can be sought by a bhikkhu. [Thinking:] 'A half month is what remains of the hot season,' [after] having made [it, it] can be worn. If earlier than [what is reckoned as] 'One month is what remains of the hot season,' he should seek robe-cloth for the rain's bathing-cloth, [and] [if] earlier than [what is reckoned as] 'A half month is what remains of the hot season,' he should wear [it], [this is a case] involving expiation with forfeiture."6

    The renderings agree on the two times and differ on the cloth's name and on one word. Brahmāli's "rainy-season robe" is Thanissaro's "rains-bathing cloth", and Ñāṇatusita sets out what it is: the cloth a monk wears so as not to be naked when he bathes in the falling rain, where Horner thought it a robe worn in the rains to keep the others dry.7 The rule's katvā, "having made", is kept by Thanissaro and Ñāṇatusita, and as "sew" by Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation; his recitation and Ajahn Brahm's paraphrase do not carry it.8 Ajahn Brahm turns the time remaining into the whole of each permitted season. None of this changes what the rule requires.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    This rule protects a monk's modesty, by keeping an allowance to the season it was given for. The Khandhaka tells how the Buddha told the monks to bathe in a heavy rain, and how Visākhā's servant, sent to call them to a meal, saw them bathing naked and thought there were no monks in the monastery, "just Ājīvaka ascetics". Visākhā asked to give rainy-season robes to the Saṅgha for as long as she lived, "Nakedness is gross, disgusting, and repulsive", and the Buddha allowed them.9 The group of six sought and wore theirs so early that when the rains came they had nothing to bathe in and went back to bathing naked.10

    The rule holds two offences, seeking at the wrong time and wearing at the wrong time, each with its own factors; Thanissaro, reporting the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, and Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual both divide it so.11​ 12 Most of it is now, in Thanissaro's word, academic: monks who use a bathing cloth determine it as a cloth for requisites, which takes it outside this rule.13 The factors for the two offences overlap, so each label below takes the seeking first and the wearing second.

    Object

    For the first offence, material for a rains cloth; for the second, a rains cloth he has.14​ 15

    Effort

    Seeking. The time for seeking runs from the last month of the hot season through the four months of the rains.16 Seeking is done as the Vibhaṅga scripts it. A monk goes to people who have given such cloth before and says, "It's time for the rainy-season robe," "It's the occasion for the rainy-season robe," "Other people, too, are giving cloth for the rainy-season robe"; he does not say "Give me", "Bring me", "Trade me" or "Buy me cloth for the rainy-season robe."17 A direct request of that kind to people who are not relatives and have not invited him belongs to NP 6, and hinting to people who have never given such cloth is wrong conduct, which the Commentary gives for breaking a duty.18 Relatives and those who have invited him may be asked outright in the season. Out of season even they may not: the Manual makes seeking include asking them, and Ajahn Brahm gives wrong conduct for the asking and the full offence when the cloth is received.19​ 20

    Wearing. He wears the cloth before the last half-month of the hot season, while he has his other robes and there is no danger.21 For both offences perception does not mitigate; seeking or wearing in the right time while believing it the wrong one, or unsure, is wrong conduct.22

    Result

    The seeking offence falls when he receives the cloth; the wearing offence in the wearing itself, however and whenever the cloth was got.23​ 24

    Special Cases

    The Khandhaka allows the cloth to be determined "for the four months of the rainy season, and apart from that assign it to another."25 A monk may therefore seek and sew in the last month of the hot season, and wear in its last half-month, but not yet determine; on the first day of the rains he determines the cloth; at their end he washes it and assigns it; and the next year he takes it out again in the last half-month before the rains.26 The half-month of wearing before determination is allowed meets NP 1's ten days. Thanissaro reads the allowance as an exemption from NP 1, so that the monk wears on and determines when the rains begin, and on Ajahn Brahm's account Buddhaghosa reads it so too, while citing the Kurundī's different answer, to determine the cloth within the ten days though the rains have not begun.27​ 28 The dilemma, Ajahn Brahm observes, is now of academic interest only: "The advent of modern bathrooms, one could say, threw a wet blanket on bathing naked in the rain!"29

    Bathing naked in the rain when one has a rains cloth is wrong conduct.30 Bathing naked in a river, a lake or a sauna is not, and the cloth was never meant for those.31 Where the rains are postponed for an added month, a monk who has already sought or worn his cloth commits no offence; he washes it, stores it and uses it at the right time.32

    The last exemptions, a stolen or a lost robe and an emergency, are read two ways in Thanissaro's report. The Commentary reads "robe" as the rains cloth and lets a monk who has lost his bathe naked; the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī lets a monk whose ordinary robes are gone, or who fears thieves, wear the rains cloth out of season. Thanissaro follows the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, since the exemptions normally apply to the full offence, and so do the Manual and Ajahn Brahm; I take them so.33​ 34​ 35

    The cloth itself has outlived the custom. Brahmāli knew of no one in Thailand who determined a cloth as a rains cloth; the bathing cloths offered there are used all year and determined as cloths for requisites.36 What the rule still teaches is what the cloth was for: that a monk is not seen naked, and that an allowance given for a season is kept for it.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • Dukkaṭa — hinting for a rains cloth to people who have never given one.37
    • Dukkaṭa — asking relatives or those who have invited, or hinting, out of season, before the cloth is received.38
    • Dukkaṭa — seeking or wearing in the right time while perceiving it as the wrong time, or unsure of it.39
    • Dukkaṭa — bathing naked in the rain when one has a rains cloth.40

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence if he goes looking for the cloth when there is a month left of summer or less, or wears it when there is a half-month left or less; if, after looking for or wearing it, he postpones the rainy-season residence (in which case he should wash it and store it and then use it at the right time); if his robe has been stolen or lost; if there is an emergency; if he is insane; or if he is the first offender.41

    Related Offences

    • NP 6 — a direct request to people who are not relatives and have not invited.
    • NP 10 — the same forbidden phrases, "Give me", "Bring me", "Trade me", "Buy me".
    • NP 1 — the ten days the half-month of wearing meets.
    • Pc 59 — assignment, where the cloth is kept outside the rains.
    • Pc 91 — the size of the rains cloth.
    • Pc 57 — bathing more often than once a fortnight.
