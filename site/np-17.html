    NP 17

    Story (Vatthu)

    While the Buddha was staying in the Sakyan country at the Banyan Tree Monastery near Kapilavatthu, the monks from the group of six had the nuns wash, dye, and comb wool. Because of this, the nuns neglected recitation, testing, the higher morality, the higher mind, and the higher wisdom.1

    Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī went to the Buddha and bowed down to him. The Buddha said to her, "Gotamī, I hope the nuns are heedful, energetic, and diligent?" "How could the nuns be heedful, venerable sir?" And she told him what was happening. The Buddha instructed, inspired, and gladdened her with a teaching; she bowed down, circumambulated him with her right side toward him, and left.2

    Soon afterwards the Buddha had the Saṅgha gathered and questioned the monks from the group of six: "Is it true, monks, that you do this?" "It's true, sir." "Are they your relatives?" "No." "Foolish men, people who are unrelated don't know what's appropriate and inappropriate, what's inspiring and uninspiring, in dealing with each other. And still you do this. This will affect people's confidence." And he laid down the rule.3

    Synopsis — the group-of-six monks have the nuns wash, dye, and comb their wool, and the nuns neglect their training; when Mahāpajāpatī reports it, the Buddha forbids a monk from having an unrelated nun work his wool.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "If a monk has an unrelated nun wash, dye, or comb wool, he commits an offense entailing relinquishment and confession."4

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu has an unrelated bhikkhunī wash, dye, or comb unspun wool, he incurs a nissaggiya pācittiya."5

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should any bhikkhu have wool washed, dyed, or carded by a bhikkhunī unrelated to him, it is to be forfeited and confessed."6

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 1)

    "Having wool washed, dyed or combed by a bhikkhunī who is not a relation is a nissaggiya pācittiya."7

    Ñāṇatusita

    "If any bhikkhu should have sheep's wool washed, dyed, or carded by an unrelated bhikkhunī, [this is a case] involving expiation with forfeiture."8

    The renderings agree, and differ in one verb and one word. For the third action Thanissaro and Ñāṇatusita have "carded" where Brahmāli and Ajahn Brahm have "comb"; both render vijaṭāpeyya, which Ñāṇatusita glosses "should have (the bhikkhunī) card, unravel", the working of raw wool, and the choice is one of idiom.9 The recitation alone writes "unspun" into the rule, making explicit the raw wool that the other renderings carry over from NP 16. The verbs are causative, as at NP 4, to which Ñāṇatusita refers them, and the question what "has … wash" requires is taken there and under Effort below.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    The rule keeps a monk from putting the nuns to work on his wool, and so guards their time for the training. The nuns of the Story neglected recitation, testing and the three higher trainings for the monks' wool, and the Buddha asked the group of six, "Are they your relatives?", as he had asked at NP 4, and gave the same reason: "people who are unrelated don't know what's appropriate and inappropriate, what's inspiring and uninspiring, in dealing with each other."10 Thanissaro finds the rule's reason in Mahāpajāpatī's report, the nuns kept from the training in heightened virtue, mind and discernment.11 Where NP 4's story is one of familiarity, this one is of the nuns' path.

    The factors are those of NP 4, which this rule mirrors; Thanissaro gives every explanation as identical with that rule's, with carding in place of beating.12 "Unrelated", "nun", the asking and the perception series are set out there. The two points that differ are the object and the combing.

    Object

    Wool not made into cloth or yarn, as at NP 16.13​ 14 So there is no offence in having an unrelated nun wash woollen cloth or yarn not yet used, and the non-offence clause says the same of "an unused and finished article."15​ 16 Once a woollen article has been used, the question moves: Ajahn Brahm notes that a robe or sitting mat of wool, used at least once, may bring an offence under NP 4 instead.17 Wool belonging to someone else is wrong conduct.18

    Effort and Result

    As at NP 4, with combing in place of beating: telling the nun to wash, dye or comb the wool is wrong conduct, and the full offence falls when it has been washed, or dyed, or combed.19 Any one of the three actions is enough,20 and any verbal request fulfils the "having it done", since the non-offence clause exempts a nun who works without being asked.21 Each further action adds wrong conduct, so that all three bring the full offence and two offences of wrong conduct.22 The wool is relinquished by the procedure of NP 1 and given back; it is not an unallowable thing, and he may then use it as he pleases.23​ 24

    Mahāpajāpatī's answer to the Buddha was a question: "How could the nuns be heedful, venerable sir?" A monk who asks nothing of a nun's hands leaves her the hours the Story says she lost.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • Dukkaṭa — telling her to wash, dye or comb it, before it is done; and each action beyond the first.25​ 26
    • Dukkaṭa — wool belonging to someone else; a nun ordained by the nuns' Saṅgha only; a related nun perceived as unrelated, or her kinship doubted.27

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence: if a related nun does the washing and an unrelated nun helps her; if a nun does the washing without being asked; if he has a nun wash an unused and finished article; if it is a trainee nun; if it is a novice nun; if he is insane; if he is the first offender.28

    Related Offences

    • NP 4 — the anchor: an unrelated nun washing, dyeing or beating a used robe, with the definitions, the asking and the perception series this rule shares.
    • NP 16 — the companion wool rule, from which the object comes.
    • Pc 25, 26 — giving robe-cloth to an unrelated nun and sewing a robe for one, the other rules of the family.

    A note on sourcing

    The Story, definitions, permutations and non-offence clauses are from Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation, and his Pātimokkha Manual gives the wool and the three actions. Thanissaro's BMC1 gives the referral to NP 4, the object and the rule's reason; Ajahn Brahm's Vinaya Notes gives the used woollen article and the returned wool. Ñāṇatusita is cited for his rendering and vijaṭāpeyya. The Concise BMC adds nothing, and the class on this rule is read but not cited.
