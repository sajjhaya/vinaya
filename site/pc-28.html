    Pc 28

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery. At that time the monks from the group of six were boarding boats by arrangement with nuns. People complained and criticised them: "Just as we enjoy ourselves on boats with our wives, so these Sakyan monastics make arrangements with the nuns and then enjoy themselves on boats." The monks heard the complaints, and the monks of few desires complained and criticised those monks: "How can the monks from the group of six board boats by arrangement with nuns?" They told the Buddha, who questioned them, established that it was true, and rebuked them — "Foolish men, how can you do this? This will affect people's confidence" — and laid down a preliminary ruling.1​ 2

    Soon afterwards a number of monks and nuns were travelling from Sāketa to Sāvatthī. On the way they needed to cross a river. The nuns said to the monks, "Let's cross together." "Sisters, it's not allowable for us to board a boat by arrangement with a nun. Either you cross first, or we will." "You have the higher status, venerables. Please go first." But because the nuns crossed afterwards, they were robbed and raped by bandits. When they arrived at Sāvatthī they told the nuns there what had happened. The nuns then told the monks, who in turn told the Buddha. Soon afterwards the Buddha gave a teaching and addressed the monks: "Monks, I allow you to board a boat by arrangement with a nun if it's for the purpose of crossing." He then amended the training rule.3

    Synopsis — The group of six take boat trips by arrangement with nuns and the laity say they are pleasure-boating with their wives; the Buddha forbids it, and when nuns who defer to a party of monks keeping the new rule, and follow behind, are attacked at a river crossing, he allows the arrangement for a crossing.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "If a monk boards a boat by arrangement with a nun, either to go upstream or downstream, except for the purpose of crossing, he commits an offense entailing confession."4

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu travels on a boat by arrangement with a bhikkhunī, except for the purpose of crossing over, he incurs a pācittiya."5

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should any bhikkhu, by arrangement, get in the same boat with a bhikkhunī going upstream or downstream—except to cross over to the other bank—it is to be confessed."6

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 2)

    "Going on a boat trip with a bhikkhunī, except on a ferry (i.e. directly across, from shore to shore), is an offence of Pācittiya."7

    Ñāṇatusita

    "If any bhikkhu, having made an arrangement, should embark [on a voyage] together with a bhikkhunī on the same boat, which is going up [-stream] or which is going down [-stream], except with [a boat which is] crossing over [a river], [this is a case] involving expiation."8

    The renderings agree on what is forbidden, and only the verb divides them. Brahmāli has the monk "board" the boat and Thanissaro "get in" it; Ñāṇatusita has him "embark [on a voyage]", arguing that the action intended is the journey and not the stepping aboard, and the recitation's "travels on a boat" leans the same way.9​ 10 The Vibhaṅga in fact gives both: a pācittiya on boarding, and another for each stretch travelled, discussed below under Effort. The recitation and Ajahn Brahm drop "upstream or downstream", and leave the exception, a crossing, to imply what is forbidden.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    For the sake of his own restraint and of how the laity see the Saṅgha, this rule keeps a monk from going on the water with a nun by arrangement, except to cross. The laity said of the group of six what they had said of the road: "Just as we enjoy ourselves on boats with our wives, so these Sakyan monastics make arrangements with the nuns and then enjoy themselves on boats." And the second sub-story repeats the lesson of Pc 27: the nuns, deferring to monks who kept the new rule to the letter, crossed the river after them and were attacked.11

    This rule is the lean mirror of Pc 27, and the sources say so themselves. Thanissaro gives it the preceding rule's factors, and its arrangement, perception and going as arranged, with getting into the boat in place of going; Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual refers the arrangement and the travelling back to Pc 27.12​ 13​ 14 What an arrangement is, what counts as assent, the wrong conduct for making it, the third-party case and the perception series are at Pc 27, and apply here unchanged.15​ 16 What is this rule's own sits in the object, the effort and the exception.

    Object

    A nun fully ordained by both Saṅghas is grounds for the pācittiya.17 Thanissaro points out a gap. Unlike many rules of this section, the Vibhaṅga here gives no wrong conduct for a nun ordained on one side only, and no exemption for a trainee nun or a novice nun; at Pc 27 that silence is harmless, because Pc 67 covers these three, but no rule on boats does the same, and so the omission "leaves them neither allowed nor forbidden by any rule." He suggests it may have been an oversight.18

    Effort

    The arrangement is Pc 27's, with "Let's board" for "Let's go." Then the Vibhaṅga adds three clauses of its own: if the monk boards after the nun, or the nun after the monk, or both together, he commits a pācittiya.19 Between them they cover every order in which two people can get into a boat, so the offence does not depend on who moved first. And without any phrase in the rule to define, the word-analysis then imports Pc 27's count: a pācittiya for every inhabited area passed, or in a wilderness for every half-yojana travelled.20​ 21 Thanissaro states the same, the boat's progress along the riverbank measured village by village.22 The metric equivalents differ as they do at Pc 27, which carries the account.

    The commentaries, as Thanissaro reports them, add a factor of intention, that the monk's purpose is amusement, and he sets it aside: "there is no basis for this in the Vibhaṅga."23 The origin story is about pleasure-boating, but the rule is broken by the arrangement and the voyage however sober the purpose.

    Except for the purpose of crossing. Here the two rules part. Pc 27's exception is about the road, which must be dangerous; this one is about the journey. One may cross, and one may not go up or down, and nothing about the water or its dangers enters into it.24 Ajahn Brahm's rendering builds the test in: a ferry, "directly across, from shore to shore."25 So the exception covers the one boat journey a monk and a nun are most likely to need to make together, the one the second sub-story shows was needed, and the rule reaches the journey that has no far bank as its purpose.

    The rule speaks of a river, upstream and downstream. Crossing any body of water is allowed, as the Manual and Ajahn Brahm give it from the Commentary.26​ 27 Over the sea, Thanissaro reports the commentaries divided: the Commentary extends the crossing allowance to it, so that travelling from one seaport to another by arrangement carries no penalty; the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī confines the rule to rivers altogether; and the Sub-commentary gives wrong conduct for every half-yojana at sea. Thanissaro follows that last reading, as "more in keeping with the Great Standards".28

    It is easy to miss that neither this rule nor the last has anything to do with being alone. Privacy is the concern of Pc 30, two rules on; neither rule's wording here mentions it, and neither Vibhaṅga makes it a factor.29​ 30 A crowded ferry is allowed because it is a crossing, not because it is crowded; a river cruise arranged with a nun is an offence because it is a cruise, however many others are aboard.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • An offence of wrong conduct for making the arrangement itself, on the word-analysis's closing clause, as at Pc 27.31
    • An offence of wrong conduct where the monk makes the arrangement and the nun does not express her agreement.32
    • An offence of wrong conduct where there is in fact no arrangement, but the monk perceives that there is one or is unsure.33
    • A pācittiya on boarding, whichever of the two boards first, and whether they board together.34
    • A pācittiya for every inhabited area passed, or, in a wilderness, for every half-yojana travelled.35

    Perception does not mitigate where the arrangement is real: if there is one, the full offence falls whether he perceives it, is unsure of it, or does not perceive it.36

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence: if it is for the purpose of crossing; if they board without an arrangement; if the nun has made an arrangement but he has not expressed his agreement; if they board, but not according to the arrangement; if there is an emergency; if he is insane; or if he is the first offender.37

    Related Offences

    • Pc 27 — travelling by arrangement on a country road with a nun; this rule's anchor, which carries the arrangement, the perception series and the offence unit.
    • Pc 66, Pc 67 — travelling by arrangement with a caravan known to include thieves, and with a woman; the other two members of the family, with the same arrangement formula.
    • Pc 30 — sitting alone in private with a nun; the rule that asks the question this one does not, whether anyone else was present.

Brahmāli, Vibhaṅga translation, Pc 28, definitions: "A nun: she has been given the full ordination by both Sanghas"; "By arrangement: if he makes an arrangement like this: he says, 'Let's board, Sister,' and she replies, 'Yes, let's board, venerable;' … then he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If the monk boards when the nun has already boarded, he commits an offense entailing confession. If the nun boards when the monk has already boarded, he commits an offense entailing confession. If they both board together, he commits an offense entailing confession"; "Except for the purpose of crossing: unless it is to go across." Then: "When the inhabited areas are a chicken's flight apart, then for every next inhabited area he commits an offense entailing confession. When it is an uninhabited area, a wilderness, then for every six kilometers he commits an offense entailing confession."↩︎

    32. Brahmāli, Vibhaṅga translation, Pc 28, permutations: "If there is an arrangement, and he perceives that there is, and he boards a boat with a nun, either to go upstream or downstream, except for the purpose of crossing, he commits an offense entailing confession… If the monk makes an arrangement, but the nun does not express her agreement, he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If there is no arrangement, but he perceives that there is, he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If there is no arrangement, but he is unsure of it, he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If there is no arrangement, and he does not perceive that there is, there is no offense."↩︎
