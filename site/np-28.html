    NP 28

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery. At that time a government official who was about to set out on a journey sent a message to the monks, saying, "Come, venerables, I wish to give robe-cloth to those who have completed the rainy-season residence." The monks thought, "The Buddha has allowed such robes only for those who have completed the rains residence," and being afraid of wrongdoing they did not go. That government official complained and criticised them: "How could they not come when I send a message? I'm about to set out with the army. It's hard to know whether I'll live or die."

    The monks heard the complaints of that government official, and they told the Buddha. Soon afterwards the Buddha gave a teaching and addressed the monks: "Monks, I allow you to receive a haste-cloth, and then store it."

    When they heard about this, monks received haste-cloths and stored them beyond the robe season, keeping them in bundles on a bamboo robe rack. While walking about the dwellings, Venerable Ānanda saw those cloths, and he asked the monks, "Whose cloths are these?"

    "They're our haste-cloths."

    "But how long have you stored them?"

    They told him. Ānanda then complained and criticised them: "How could these monks receive haste-cloths and then store them beyond the robe season?" After rebuking those monks in many ways, Ānanda told the Buddha. Soon afterwards he had the Saṅgha gathered and questioned the monks: "Is it true, monks, that there are monks who do this?"

    "It's true, sir."

    The Buddha rebuked them — "How could those foolish men do this? This will affect people's confidence" — and laid down the rule.1

    Synopsis — An official setting out with the army wishes to give robe-cloth before the rains are over, and the Buddha allows monks to receive such haste-cloth and store it; when monks store it beyond the robe season, he makes storing it beyond that season an offence entailing relinquishment.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "When there are ten days left to the Kattika full moon that ends the first rainy-season residence and a haste-cloth is given to a monk, he may receive it if he regards it as urgent. He may then store it until the end of the robe season. If he stores it beyond that, he commits an offense entailing relinquishment and confession."2

    Condensed recitation translation — "During the last ten days before the pavāraṇā ceremony, a bhikkhu may receive an urgently offered robe. If he then keeps it beyond the robe season, he incurs a nissaggiya pācittiya."3

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Ten days prior to the third-month Kattika full moon, should robe-cloth offered in urgency accrue to a bhikkhu, he is to accept it if he regards it as offered in urgency. Once he has accepted it, he may keep it throughout the robe season. Beyond that, it is to be forfeited and confessed."4

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 1)

    "Keeping an acceka-cīvara beyond the end of the Robe-Season is a nissaggiya pācittiya."5

    Ñāṇatusita

    "For the ten days coming up to the three-month Kattikā full moon: if extraordinary robe [-cloth] should become available to a bhikkhu, [then] after considering [it as] extraordinary [robe-cloth, it] can be accepted by a bhikkhu, having been accepted, [it] is to be put aside until the occasion of the robe-season; if he should put [it] aside for more than that, [this is a case] involving expiation with forfeiture."6

    The renderings agree on the rule; they differ on the name of the cloth and the day. Acceka is a contracted form of accāyika, "not suffering delay", and Ñāṇatusita renders it "extraordinary" because the word carries both the donor's circumstances and the unusual time of the gift, where Brahmāli's "haste-cloth" and Thanissaro's "offered in urgency" each take one strand.7 Brahmāli's recitation names the pavāraṇā ceremony in place of the Kattika full moon: the two are the same day, and he explained in class that hardly anyone would know what the Kattika moon meant.8

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    A gift accepted out of compassion is not to become a store: the rule keeps a monk from making it one, and keeps the gift where the donor meant it, with the Saṅgha that kept the rains. The official wanted to give before the season because, in his words, "It's hard to know whether I'll live or die", and the Buddha allowed the monks to receive the cloth and store it; the monks then kept it in bundles on a bamboo rack beyond the robe season, and the rule sets the limit to the allowance.9 It adds no penalty NP 1 does not already carry, as Ajahn Brahm notes with the Samantapāsādikā; the allowance is the rule's real addition, and the limit makes plain that haste-cloth is extra cloth like any other once the season ends.10

    In Thanissaro's analysis the offence has two factors: object, robe-cloth offered in urgency, and effort, keeping it past the end of the robe season.11 Brahmāli's Pātimokkha Manual sets out seven, which fall under the same two.

    Object

    Haste-cloth is robe-cloth given in the last ten days before the pavāraṇā ceremony by a donor with a pressing reason. The Manual counts the days as less than ten full days before dawn on the day of the great pavāraṇā, the third full moon of the rains, and Thanissaro states the same window as the fifth to the fifteenth of the waxing moon.12​ 13 The Vibhaṅga names the donors: one about to set out with the army or on a journey, one who is sick, one who is pregnant, one without faith who acquires it, one without confidence who acquires it, who sends word that he wishes to give a robe to those who have completed the rains.14 Brahmāli read the list as examples, so that any donor with a real reason for urgency qualifies; new-found faith, which puts no life in danger, already shows how wide the door is.15 The monk receives it "establishing the perception of it as a haste-cloth", which Thanissaro reads as marking it before he puts it aside.16​ 17 Thanissaro finds the mark's likeliest purpose in the cloth's clock: other cloth received in these days can run past the season under NP 1 or NP 3, haste-cloth cannot, and the mark keeps a monk from forgetting which is which.18

    The cloth is a Rains-residence gift, shared out among the monks and novices who kept the rains in that monastery and given to no visitor before the season ends; Ajahn Brahm advises against sharing it out before the rains are over, since a share belongs only to one who completes the residence. Cloth offered to a particular monk before the rains end may be accepted, but it is not haste-cloth.19 The rule's clock runs on cloth that has been shared out: once distributed to a monk, in Ajahn Brahm's words, haste-cloth "becomes the same as an 'extra cloth'".20

    Effort

    The monk keeps the cloth beyond the robe season without determining or assigning it.21 The robe season is NP 1's: for one who has not taken part in the kaṭhina, the last month of the rains; for one who has, five months, that month and the cold season. It ends early if the Saṅgha forgoes its privileges, or if the monk leaves the monastery and gives up any intention of making a robe.22 Perception does not excuse. If the cloth is haste-cloth, the monk who keeps it past the season commits the full offence whether he knows it, doubts it, or takes it for ordinary cloth with time left under NP 3, and likewise if he thinks it determined or assigned when it is not; Ajahn Brahm adds that a monk whose kaṭhina privileges lapse without his realising it is caught all the same.23​ 24​ 25

    How long, at most, may haste-cloth be kept? Ajahn Brahm reports from the Samantapāsādikā a case at the seam with NP 1: robe-material a monk receives for himself on the first day of the window reaches NP 1's tenth dawn on the pavāraṇā day itself, a dawn before the robe season begins, and must be dealt with by then.26 The Manual takes this to be said of haste-cloth and answers that the rule itself lets haste-cloth be stored to the season's end.27 The two seem not to be at odds. Ajahn Brahm's case is cloth received "for himself", and cloth offered to a particular monk, he says, is not haste-cloth at all.28​ 29 Haste-cloth, on either account, may be kept to the season's end, as the rule says.

    The official gave in haste because he might not come back. The rule honours that haste and sets a date on it; past the season, the cloth is no longer a donor's urgent gift but a bundle on a rack.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • Dukkaṭa — using cloth that should be relinquished, without first relinquishing it.30
    • Dukkaṭa — cloth that is not haste-cloth, perceived as haste-cloth or doubted; on Thanissaro's reading of the Commentary to NP 1, for using it unrelinquished after the season.31​ 32

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence if within the robe season the haste-cloth has been determined, assigned to another, given away, lost, destroyed, burned, stolen, or taken on trust; if he is insane; or if he is the first offender.33 Taking on trust is treated at NP 1.

    Related Offences

    • NP 1 — the anchor for extra cloth, determination, assignment, the robe season and relinquishment, all borrowed here.
    • NP 3 — out-of-season cloth, whose extension haste-cloth is denied.
    • NP 24 — the other rule built as an early window onto a season, allowing an early gift and limiting how long it is kept.
    • Pc 32, Pc 33, Pc 46 — the robe-giving period as a standing exception.

Thus the Vibhaṅga states that if, at the end of the robe season, one perceives a piece of robe-cloth offered in urgency as something else---say, as ordinary out-of-season cloth---and keeps it for the amount of time allowed for ordinary out-of-season cloth under NP 3, one commits the full offense all the same"; "Arguing from the Commentary's explanation of the similar situation discussed under NP 1, the dukkaṭa here would be for using the cloth without having forfeited it after the robe season is ended."↩︎
