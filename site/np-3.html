    NP 3

    Story (Vatthu)

    At Sāvatthī, in Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery, a monk was given robe-cloth outside the robe season. While he was making the robe he realised there was not enough cloth. He lifted it up and smoothed it out, again and again — sprinkling it with water, stepping on it with his feet, and lifting and holding it up with his hands to rub it against his back; but when it dried in the sun it returned to its old size, and he would begin again.1

    Walking about the dwellings, the Buddha saw the monk doing this and went up to him. "What are you doing, monk?" "Sir, I've been given this out-of-season robe-cloth, but it's not enough to make a robe. That's why I lift it up and smooth it out again and again." "Are you expecting to receive more cloth?" "I am." Soon afterwards the Buddha gave a teaching and told the monks: "I allow you to keep out-of-season robe-cloth if you are expecting to receive more."2

    When they heard this, some monks kept out-of-season robe-cloth for more than a month, storing it in bundles on a bamboo robe rack. Walking about the dwellings, Venerable Ānanda saw the cloth and asked whose it was. "It's our out-of-season robe-cloth, which we're keeping because we're expecting more." "But how long have you kept it?" "More than a month." Ānanda complained: "How can these monks keep out-of-season robe-cloth for more than a month?" He told the Buddha, who questioned the monks, rebuked them — "How can those foolish men keep out-of-season robe-cloth for more than a month? This will affect people's confidence" — and laid down the rule.3

    Synopsis — a monk worries at a too-small piece of cloth, smoothing it as if he could stretch it into a robe; the Buddha, seeing him, allows out-of-season cloth to be held while more is awaited — and then, when monks stockpile it past a month, caps the holding at thirty days.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "When his robe is finished and the robe season has ended, if out-of-season robe-cloth is offered to a monk, he may receive it if he wishes. If he receives it, he should quickly make a robe. If there is not enough cloth, but he is expecting more, he should keep it at most one month to make up the lack. If he keeps it longer than that, then even if he expects more cloth, he commits an offense entailing relinquishment and confession."4

    Condensed recitation translation — "When a bhikkhu does not have robe-season privileges, if he receives insufficient robe-cloth but is expecting more, he may keep it for one month at most. If he exceeds that, he incurs a nissaggiya pācittiya."5

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "When a bhikkhu has finished his robe and the frame is dismantled (his kathina privileges are ended): Should out-of-season robe-cloth accrue to him, he may accept it if he so desires. Having accepted it, he is to make it up immediately (into a cloth requisite). If it should not be enough (§), he may lay it aside for a month at most when he has an expectation for filling the lack. If he should keep it beyond that, even when he has an expectation (for further cloth), it is to be forfeited and confessed."6

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 1)

    "When a monk has received an 'out of season cloth', which is not enough to make a robe but he expects to get more cloth later, then he may keep that 'out of season cloth' up to thirty days. Should he keep such an 'out of season cloth' beyond thirty days, except when the 'end of the rainy-season privileges' or the 'Kaṭhina privileges' still apply, he incurs a nissaggiya pācittiya."7

    Ñāṇatusita

    "When the robe [-cloth] is finished by a bhikkhu, when the kaṭhina [-frame-privileges] have been withdrawn, if out-of-season robe [-cloth] should become available to a bhikkhu, by a bhikkhu who is wishing [so, it] can be accepted; having accepted [it, it] is to be made very quickly. If [the robe-cloth] should not be [enough for] the completion [of the robe], [then] for a month at the most that robe [-cloth] can be put aside by that bhikkhu for the completion of the deficiency [of robe-cloth], when there is an expectation [that he will get more robe-cloth]; if he should put [it] aside more than that, even when there is an expectation [that he will get more robe-cloth], [this is a case] involving expiation with forfeiture."8

    The renderings agree. The full rule says three things the condensed ones drop: that receiving the cloth is optional, that it must be made up "quickly", which the Vibhaṅga defines as within ten days, and that the expectation of more does not save cloth kept past the month.9 The recitation and Ajahn Brahm keep only the month and the expectation, which is where the rule's force lies, and both put the robe season's privileges in place of the opening clause, as at NP 1. The ten days are the link to NP 1, and they return under Effort.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    This rule keeps a monk restrained with cloth: it gives him time to gather enough for a robe, and no more. The Buddha met a monk trying to stretch a too-small piece into a robe and allowed him to keep it while more was expected; when monks then left such cloth in bundles on a rack for longer than a month, he set the month as the limit.10 The allowance served a monk in real need, and the limit kept it from becoming a store.

    The rule is a single exception to NP 1's ten days and takes everything else from it: the opening clause, the conditions that make a piece robe-cloth, and the relinquishment.11 Thanissaro gives it two factors, object and effort.12

    Object

    Out-of-season robe-cloth that is not enough for the requisite a monk has in mind, while he expects more. In-season cloth is cloth given in the robe season, the last month of the rains for one who has not taken part in the robe-making ceremony and the five months for one who has; out-of-season cloth is cloth given in the other eleven months, or seven, from whatever source, the Saṅgha, a group, a relative, a friend, rags, or his own property.13 Cloth given in the season also counts as out of season if the donors designate it so.14 Thanissaro gives two reasons a donor might: insufficient in-season cloth cannot be held for this rule's month, so a late gift designated out of season gives the monk the extra time; and in-season cloth given to a Community may be shared only among those who spent the rains there, so designating it lets visitors share, as donors once did for shabbily dressed visiting nuns.15​ 16 The expectation can be of anything the monk may properly get, a gift, rags he is on the lookout for, or cloth from his own resources.17 If the cloth is already enough, this rule does not apply, and the cloth is NP 1's extra cloth with ten days.18

    Effort

    Keeping the cloth past the thirtieth dawn after it was received, unless the season's privileges are in force. Days are counted by dawns, as at NP 1.19​ 20

    When the expected cloth comes, two limits run, the month on the first cloth and NP 1's ten days on the new, and the robe must be made within whichever ends first. The Vibhaṅga spells it out day by day: cloth arriving within the first nineteen days leaves ten days; on the twentieth, nine; on the twenty-eighth, one; and on the twenty-ninth the cloth must be determined, assigned or given away that very day, or it is subject to relinquishment at the thirtieth dawn.21​ 22 If the new cloth is different from the first, a monk need not combine them and may wait for more while the first cloth's month lasts; the Commentary adds that he may determine the poorer of the two as requisite cloth, and if the new cloth is the better, start a fresh month from it.23​ 24

    The month depends on the expectation. Thanissaro reports the Sub-commentary's ruling that if, after the first ten days, the monk gives up any expectation of more, he must determine, assign or give away the cloth before the next dawn, or fall under NP 1.25 Cloth received shortly before the robe season starts its count at once, stops through the season, and resumes when it ends.26

    Perception does not excuse. Kept beyond the month, the cloth is to be relinquished whether the monk knows it, is unsure, or thinks it within time; kept within it while he thinks it beyond, or is unsure, there is wrong conduct.27

    The rule seldom arises now. As Thanissaro notes, insufficient cloth can be determined as requisite cloth or assigned until the rest arrives.28 The rule's own sense is that cloth received is made up quickly, which the Vibhaṅga defines as within ten days.29 What the month allows is a robe on its way, not cloth put by.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • Dukkaṭa — using cloth that should be relinquished before relinquishing it.30
    • Dukkaṭa — thinking the cloth kept beyond the month, or being unsure, when it has not been; by Thanissaro, for using it.31​ 32

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence if within the month the cloth has been determined, assigned to another, given away, lost, destroyed, burned, stolen or taken on trust; if he is insane; or if he is the first offender.33 The rule does not apply while the season's privileges are in force.

    Related Offences

    • NP 1 — the ten days this rule extends, and the rule cloth returns to once it is enough or the expectation is gone.
    • NP 2 — the same opening clause.
    • NP 24 — whose Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, Thanissaro reports, allows out-of-season cloth, unlike insufficient in-season cloth, to be held for the month.
    • Pc 59 — assignment, one of the ways insufficient cloth is now kept.

    Related Suttas

    • AN 4.28 — "a bhikkhu is content with any kind of robe, and he speaks in praise of contentment with any kind of robe"; the month is for gathering enough for a robe, and no longer.34
    • AN 4.27 — "A rag-robe is a trifle among robes, easily gained and blameless"; the robe pieced from what comes to hand, the opposite of cloth left in bundles on a rack.35
