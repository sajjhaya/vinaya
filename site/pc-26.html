    Pc 26

    Story (Vatthu)

    The Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Monastery. At that time Venerable Udāyī had become skilled in making robes. A certain nun went to him and said, "Venerable, would you please sew me a robe?"

    He sewed a robe for her, well-dyed and beautifully made, and he drew a picture in the middle of it. He then folded it and put it aside. When the nun came and asked where the robe was, he said, "Now, sister, take this robe as it's folded and put it aside. When the Sangha of nuns goes to the instruction, then put it on and follow right behind the other nuns."

    The nun did just that. People complained and criticised her: "How indecent these nuns are, what shameless scoundrels, in drawing pictures on their robes!" The nuns asked her who had done it, and she said, "Venerable Udāyī." They said, "This sort of work would not even make an indecent, shameless scoundrel look good, let alone Venerable Udāyī."

    The nuns told the monks, and the monks of few desires complained and criticised him: "How could Venerable Udāyī sew a robe for a nun?" They told the Buddha, who questioned him, established that the nun was not a relative of his, and rebuked him — "Foolish man, a man and a woman who are unrelated don't know what's appropriate and inappropriate, what's inspiring and uninspiring, in dealing with each other. And still you did this. This will affect people's confidence" — and laid down the training rule.1

    Synopsis — Udāyī, a skilled robe-maker, sews a robe for a nun and works a picture into the middle of it, then arranges for her to wear it in the procession to the instruction; the Buddha forbids a monk to sew a robe, or have one sewn, for an unrelated nun.

    Translation (Mātikā)

    Brahmāli

    Full Vibhaṅga translation — "If a monk sews a robe for an unrelated nun, or has one sewn, he commits an offense entailing confession."2

    Condensed recitation translation — "If a bhikkhu sews a robe for an unrelated bhikkhunī, or has one sewn, he incurs a pācittiya."3

    Thanissaro (BMC1)

    "Should any bhikkhu sew robe-cloth or have it sewn for a bhikkhunī unrelated to him, it is to be confessed."4

    Brahm (Vinaya Notes, vol. 2)

    "Sewing a robe, or having it sewn, for a bhikkhunī who is not a relation, is an offence of Pācittiya."5

    Ñāṇatusita

    "If any bhikkhu should sew a robe or should have a robe sewn for an unrelated bhikkhunī, [this is a case] involving expiation."6

    One word divides these renderings, and it is the object: Thanissaro has the monk sewing "robe-cloth", and the others have him sewing "a robe". The difference is a reading of the Vibhaṅga's definition rather than a choice of English, and it is taken up under Object. The two verbs, sewing and having sewn, are the other thing to notice: the renderings agree on them, but they carry very different counts, discussed below under Effort.

    Explanation (Padabhājanīya)

    Needlework binds a monk to the nun he sews for more closely than a gift of cloth, and this rule keeps him from it where she is not his relative, for his own restraint and for the faith of those who watch the Saṅgha. The Buddha's rebuke to Udāyī gives the reason in the words he used at Pc 25: "a man and a woman who are unrelated don't know what's appropriate and inappropriate, what's inspiring and uninspiring, in dealing with each other … This will affect people's confidence."7 Udāyī's skill had put a picture into the middle of the robe and the nun into the procession to the instruction wearing it, and it was she whom people called shameless.

    This rule and Pc 25 are usually read as a pair, but they are siblings, not mirrors. Pc 25 took its object and its exception from NP 5; this rule takes nothing from NP 5 and has no exception at all. What the two share is "unrelated" and "nun", which both take from NP 4.8 Thanissaro gives three factors, effort, object and intention, the intention standing as a factor of its own, I take it, because the sewing may be done long before the robe reaches anyone.9

    Effort

    Here the Vibhaṅga is at its most exacting. A monk who sews the robe himself commits a pācittiya for every stitch.10 A monk who asks another to sew it commits a pācittiya for the asking; if he asks only once, there is no more however much is sewn, and Brahmāli's endnote gives the Commentary adding that even if the other finishes the whole robe, there is just the one.11​ 12 Thanissaro reads the clause as giving a second pācittiya when the other does as asked.13 So the monk who sews with his own hands may incur hundreds of offences, and the monk who asks someone else once incurs one, or two on Thanissaro's reading.

    Since the offence is counted by the stitch, it lies in the sewing, not in the giving. A monk who sews a robe intending it for an unrelated nun has offended before she has seen it, and would have offended had he changed his mind and never given it. Thanissaro reports the Commentary extending the rule to shared work: where one monk sews a robe for a nun related to him and another monk, not related to her, helps, the helper incurs a pācittiya for every stitch he sews; the Sub-commentary, he adds, exempts him if he does not know whom the robe is for.14

    Object

    The object is a robe. Sewing anything else for an unrelated nun, a bag or a cover, is no offence, since the non-offence clause exempts any requisite apart from a robe.15 What counts as a robe is less settled. Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga gives the definition as "one of the six kinds of robes", without the minimum size Pc 25 carries, and his Pātimokkha Manual takes the object as "any wearable robe", noting that the size clause is absent and that a rule about sewing seems naturally to mean a complete robe.16​ 17 Thanissaro reads the definition as Pc 25's, robe-cloth of the minimum size, and then narrows it by the non-offence clause to cloth being sewn into a robe, which brings him to the same practical line by another route.18 Ajahn Brahm confines the object to "any one of a bhikkhunī's five robes", citing the Cullavagga's ordination procedure, which names her five robes.19 That passage says what a nun's set of robes is, not what the word means in this rule, and I follow the wider reading, which keeps to the word-analysis and on which Brahmāli and Thanissaro arrive together.

    Intention

    The robe must be meant for a nun who is unrelated in the sense of NP 4 and fully ordained by both Saṅghas.20 A nun ordained on the nuns' side only is wrong conduct; a related nun, a trainee nun or a novice nun is no offence.21​ 22 Perception does not mitigate, and the series is Pc 25's: if she is in fact unrelated, the full offence falls whether she is taken as unrelated, doubted, or taken to be related. Thanissaro refers the reader again to Pc 4, where the series is set out.23​ 24

    The rule was laid down over the picture Udāyī worked into the robe, which Brahmāli's endnote, quoting the Commentary, describes as a man and a woman in intercourse, and it says nothing about pictures at all.25 It forbids the sewing. A monk who sews an unrelated nun a plain robe, well made and given with the best of intentions, commits the full offence with every stitch, and unlike the cloth of the rule before, the labour has no exchange to cure it.

    Derived Offences (Āpattibheda)

    • A dukkaṭa where the robe is sewn, or got sewn, for a nun ordained on one side only, in the nuns' Saṅgha but not the monks'.26​ 27
    • A dukkaṭa where the nun is in fact related but the monk perceives her as unrelated, or is unsure whether she is related.28

    Exemptions (Anāpatti)

    There is no offence: if the nun is related; if he sews any requisite other than a robe, or has it sewn; if she is a trainee nun; if she is a novice nun; if he is insane; or if he is the first offender.29 There is likewise no offence where the nun is in fact related and the monk perceives her as related.30 The list is shorter than Pc 25's: there is no exchange, no taking on trust and no borrowing.

    Related Offences

    • Pc 25 — giving robe-cloth to an unrelated nun, except in exchange; this rule's sibling. Both take "unrelated" and "nun" from NP 4. Cloth there, labour here, and the object shifts with it, from robe-cloth to a robe.
    • NP 4 — having an unrelated nun wash, dye or beat a used robe; the anchor of the family, and the only rule the Manual refers this one to. There the nun labours for the monk, here the monk for the nun, and both are offences.
    • NP 26 — getting weavers to weave robe-cloth, where the request alone is usually penalty-free; this rule is the stated exception, putting a full pācittiya on the request itself.
    • Pc 4 — where the perception series this rule follows is set out: a monk who believes the nun to be a relative is no better off for believing it.

    A note on sourcing

    The Story is from Brahmāli's Vibhaṅga translation alone, at its own level of statement: it says Udāyī "drew a picture in the middle of it", and the content of the picture, which is the Samantapāsādikā's as Brahmāli's endnote reports it, is given in the Explanation rather than narrated. Ajahn Brahm's retelling makes the nun Udāyī's former wife, puts the picture on the back of the robe and has it folded to conceal the design; none of this is in the Vibhaṅga and none is carried. Brahmāli's recitation, the Manual and the class transcript (session 024, 17 May 2012), Thanissaro's BMC1, Ajahn Brahm's Vinaya Notes, the Concise BMC and Ñāṇatusita were checked. Thanissaro, BMC1, Pc 26: "The full offense here has three factors. 1) Effort: One sews—or gets someone else to sew— 2) Object: a robe 3) Intention: for the sake of a bhikkhunī unrelated to oneself." Effort: "If one gets someone else to sew it, there is a pācittiya in giving the command or making the request, and another pācittiya when the other person does as commanded/requested, no matter how many robe-cloths he/she sews." Intention: "An unrelated bhikkhunī who has received only her first ordination, from the bhikkhunīs, is grounds for a dukkaṭa… Perception as to whether the bhikkhunī is actually one's relative is not a mitigating factor here (see Pc 4). The Commentary states that if Bhikkhu X is sewing robe-cloth for a bhikkhunī related to him, and Bhikkhu Y—who is not related to her—helps him sew it, Bhikkhu Y incurs a pācittiya for every stitch he sews in the cloth. The Sub-commentary adds, though, that if Bhikkhu Y does not know that the cloth is for the bhikkhunī, he is exempt from the offense."↩︎

    28. Brahmāli, Vibhaṅga translation, Pc 26, permutations: "If she is unrelated and he perceives her as such, and he sews her a robe or has one sewn, he commits an offense entailing confession… If he sews a robe, or has one sewn, for a nun who is fully ordained only on one side, he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If she is related, but he perceives her as unrelated, he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If she is related, but he is unsure of it, he commits an offense of wrong conduct. If she is related and he perceives her as such, there is no offense."↩︎

    29. Brahmāli, Vibhaṅga translation, Pc 26, non-offences: "There is no offense: if she is related; if he sews any requisite apart from a robe, or has it sewn; if it is a trainee nun; if it is a novice nun; if he is insane; if he is the first offender."↩︎
